# 01 — Opening and player journey

## The proposed starting premise

The player is a young successor to a Deccan clan. Their father was a capable warlord and noble who served more than one major ruler over his life, built a respected local network, and maintained both friendships and feuds. After the father's death or political transition (exact event OPEN), the Sultan does not entrust the young heir with the father's former high authority. A more experienced lord receives important responsibility; the heir retains a modest ancestral base: some villages, a town or market center, basic fortifications and limited clan property, but not a royal estate or an empire.

This creates an immediate reason to play: prove worth to the Sultan, rebuild the clan, negotiate with neighbors, seek another patron, or pursue independence. The father’s old friends may help; rivals may see the transition as an opportunity to erase the clan's influence.

**Narrative details not final:** starting date, exact inheritance, father’s fate, identity of the initial Sultan/faction, exact number of villages/town/forts, whether the opening is playable or cinematic, and which rival is the initial antagonist.

## Opening sequence — to be designed

Candidate flow:
1. Title / new game / settings / accessibility.
2. Choose a campaign setup (whether to choose historical start, difficulty, or sandbox rules is OPEN).
3. Briefly establish the clan, inheritance and current political situation.
4. Show the starting territory on the strategic map and explain the immediate pressures.
5. Give the player a small set of meaningful first actions rather than presenting every system at once.
6. Allow the player to inspect a settlement, household/workforce, treasury, army, fortification and neighboring lords.
7. Introduce the first dilemma: a practical local need and a political/military opportunity or threat.

The sequence above is a hypothesis only. Do not implement until the user has described the desired first minutes in more detail.

## Possible long-term player paths

- Trusted vassal / senior general.
- Loyal but increasingly autonomous regional lord.
- Independent king or founder of a kingdom.
- Power broker who supports or replaces a superior ruler.
- Merchant-oriented regional power.
- Mercenary commander.
- Bandit / outlaw leader with fragile control over land and people.
- Defeated claimant who rebuilds from a reduced position—or reaches a game-over condition.

These are desired possibilities, not all guaranteed v1 mechanics. Define what actually distinguishes the paths in gameplay before promising them as shipped features.

## First-session usability principle

The player should be able to answer, at a glance:
- What do I own and control?
- What is currently going well or badly?
- What do my people need?
- What can I build, afford, recruit, or change?
- Who are my closest allies and threats?
- What could happen if I take the next action?
